# Concept — Capital social

**RO:** capital social · **EN:** share capital / registered capital

## Definition
The aggregate value of members’ contributions, expressed in lei, recorded in the constitutive act and in the **trade register** ([[ONRC]]). Legally it is not a pot of money — it is a **figure on the liabilities side** representing the capital base against which distributions and certain reorganisation rules are measured.

## What it actually does
1. **Creditor protection (partial).** Capital maintenance fences a floor of assets from shareholders; modern practice also relies on solvency tests and directors’ duties.
2. **Allocation of rights (primary day-to-day function).** Votes, profits, and liquidation surplus track capital participations unless the constitutive act validly provides otherwise.

## Mechanics
- **Contributions** — cash or in kind; in-kind requires valuation; over-valuation is a liability vector
- **Increase** — new contributions or capitalisation of reserves; pre-emption rights are the minority flashpoint
- **Reduction** — creditor-protective procedure with notice / objection rights
- **Net assets below capital** — statutory duty to act (recapitalise, reduce, reorganise, dissolve) — the figure that makes capital “live”

## Notes / conclusions
> Do not read registered capital as a measure of substance. Read it as (a) a rights-allocation key and (b) a trigger level for net-assets duties. For creditworthiness, go to the financial statements.
