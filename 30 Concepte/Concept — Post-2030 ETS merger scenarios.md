# Concept — Post-2030 ETS merger scenarios

**EN:** post-2030 ETS 1 / ETS 2 integration pathways · **RO:** scenarii de integrare ETS 1 / ETS 2 post-2030

Under **Article 30i** of Directive 2003/87/EC, the Commission must report by **2031** on whether to merge ETS 1 and ETS 2 into a single unified carbon market.

Parent: [[Concept — ETS 1 vs ETS 2]] · [[EU ETS — sinteza pietei carbon]]

---

## Economic argument for integration

Classic equimarginal principle: a single price signal drives abatement wherever cheapest across the economy.

- **Unified price signal** — equal incentive for steel mill vs diesel truck
- **Deeper liquidity** — industrial buyers + fuel distributors in one pool
- **Eliminates cross-sector arbitrage** — e.g. industrial process heat (ETS 1) vs commercial building heating (ETS 2)

---

## Structural friction

### I. Price elasticity asymmetry

| ETS 1 sectors | ETS 2 sectors |
|---|---|
| Elastic demand (fuel switching, renewables) | Inelastic demand (retrofits, EV transition take years) |
| Low–medium short-term abatement cost | High short-term abatement cost |

**Spillover risk:** inelastic ETS 2 demand could drive unified EUA prices to levels crushing industrial competitiveness before green steel / CCUS reach scale.

### II. Distributional spillover
ETS 1 price shocks hit corporate balance sheets. In a merged market, the same shock passes through to **household fuel and heating bills** — intense political pushback.

### III. Regulatory entry-point mismatch
ETS 1: ~10,000 downstream installations. ETS 2: upstream fuel distributors. Merger requires harmonised MRV to prevent double counting.

---

## Four integration pathways

| Option | Architecture | Advantage | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Full immediate merger** | Single cap · single price · shared MSR | Maximum efficiency | Price spikes hit households or bankrupt industry |
| **2. One-way fungibility** | ETS 2 can buy ETS 1 EUAs; not reverse | Caps ETS 2 upside | Drains ETS 1 liquidity |
| **3. Banded / corridor** | Cross-trading only when spread > threshold (e.g. €30/t) | Gradual convergence | Regulatory complexity · gaming |
| **4. Permanent ring-fencing** | Separate caps · MSRs · price rules forever | Isolates consumer from industrial shocks | Structural price inefficiency |

---

## Market consensus (as of conversation capture)

Full merger by 2031 is **unlikely and politically risky**. Expected trajectory:

1. **2027–2030:** full separation — ETS 2 price discovery under €45/t ceiling
2. **Post-2030 review:** capped cross-market transfers or limited one-way fungibility
3. **Towards 2040:** full merger only when heat pumps and EVs reach mass penetration (ETS 2 elasticity rises)
