# Concept — Furnizor de ultimă instanță

**RO:** furnizor de ultimă instanță (FUI) · **EN:** supplier of last resort

## Definition
A supplier **designated to take over consumers who would otherwise be left without supply** — because their supplier lost its licence, went insolvent, exited, or because the consumer never actively chose one. The mechanism that makes it safe to liberalise an essential service.

> MD parallel label: *furnizor de ultimă opțiune* (FUO). Same function, different statutory vocabulary.

## The problem it solves
Liberalisation means suppliers can fail. In electricity/gas, failure cannot mean an unserved household. The law inserts a **default counterparty**: supply transfers automatically; commercial consequences are sorted afterwards.

## How it works
- **Designation** — by or under [[ANRE]] procedure for defined categories / areas; typically a **public service obligation**
- **Automatic transfer** — on the trigger event, consumers pass to the FUI **without needing to contract**
- **Price** — regulated or constrained so as not to advantage last-resort supply over the competitive market
- **Temporary character** — bridge toward a chosen supplier; often time-limited expectations
- **Compensation / recovery** — where the obligation imposes net cost, the PSO / tariff framework provides recovery rules

## Distinguish from universal service
| Concept | Role |
|---|---|
| **Universal service** | Standing right of household/small consumers to be supplied at specified quality/price |
| **FUI** | Contingency mechanism when normal supply fails |

Often the same undertaking discharges both — different triggers, different legal bases.

## Notes / conclusions
> When a supplier fails, the legally interesting questions are about **who bears the cost of the transferred portfolio** and whether the FUI can recover it — not about whether consumers stay connected (they do).
