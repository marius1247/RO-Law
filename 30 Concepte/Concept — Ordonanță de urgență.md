# Concept — Ordonanță de urgență

**RO:** ordonanță de urgență (OUG) · **EN:** emergency ordinance (Government)

## Definition
A normative act adopted by the **Government** under **[[Constitutia Romaniei (text)|Constituția]] art. 115** in extraordinary situations whose regulation cannot be postponed. An OUG has **force of law** upon publication, pending parliamentary approval, and is the distinctive feature of Romania’s legislative practice compared with Moldova’s cleaner Parliament-led cascade.

## Constitutional constraints (art. 115)
- Extraordinary situation; regulation cannot be postponed
- Must contain reasons for urgency
- **Cannot** affect certain reserved domains (constitutional review polices the edges — fundamental institutions, rights in specified ways, etc.)
- Submitted to Parliament for **approval or rejection**; Parliament may amend via the approving law
- Organic-law domains require the approving act to observe organic majorities

→ [[Ierarhia actelor normative]] (`forta_juridica` **4**) · [[Procesul legislativ]]

## Why it matters in this vault
Energy market design, consumer-price interventions, and tax measures are routinely delivered by OUG. Examples already touching the corpus:
- Amending waves to [[Legea 123-2012 — energia electrica si gazele naturale (text)|L123/2012]]
- Organisation of [[ANRE]] via [[OUG 33-2007 — organizarea ANRE (text)|OUG 33/2007]] (approved / amended by later laws)
- Annual / mid-year fiscal packages amending Codul fiscal

Treat a published OUG as **binding primary legislation** until rejected; then analyse transitional effects carefully.

## Distinctions
| Act | Basis | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **Lege** | Parliament (art. 73/76) | Ordinary primary route |
| **OUG** | Government art. 115 | Emergency primary route |
| **Ordonanță** (simple) | Enabling law / art. 108–115 track | Delegated legislation |
| **HG** | Art. 108 | Execution / organisation — cannot amend a statute |

## Governing provisions

## Notes / conclusions
