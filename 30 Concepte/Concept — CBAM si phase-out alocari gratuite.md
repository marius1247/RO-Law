# Concept — CBAM si phase-out alocari gratuite

**EN:** CBAM and free-allocation phase-out interaction · **RO:** interacțiunea CBAM cu eliminarea treptată a alocărilor gratuite

The most consequential structural change to EU ETS since inception: **Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM)** replaces free EUAs for energy-intensive trade-exposed sectors, maintaining a level playing field inside the EU without granting free allowances.

Parent: [[EU ETS — sinteza pietei carbon]] · formula detail: [[Concept — CBAM formula calcul certificate]]

---

## Logic

Historically, heavy industry (steel, cement, aluminium, fertilisers, hydrogen) received **free EUAs** at benchmark efficiency levels to prevent **carbon leakage** (relocating production to non-carbon-priced jurisdictions).

CBAM imposes a levy on imports equal to the EUA price (adjusted for remaining free allocation), so domestic producers face the same carbon cost as importers — without double protection (WTO compliance).

## Synchronized transition timeline (2026–2034)

Free allocation and CBAM phase-in are **mirror images** via the yearly **CBAM Factor (CF_y)**:

| Year | Free allocation remaining (CF) | CBAM financial exposure |

> **2029–2031 cliff:** phase-out accelerates sharply — largest structural shift in industrial EUA demand.

## Three impact channels

### I. Industrial EUA buying patterns
- **From sellers to buyers:** operators with surplus free EUAs become permanent auction/secondary-market buyers.
- **Working capital:** carbon hedging becomes operational treasury function, not passive compliance.
- **Forward hedging:** Dec-26 through Dec-29 futures to fix production margins.

### II. Benchmark tightening & conditionality
- **Benchmark revisions:** top 10% efficient installations set the baseline — periodic updates reduce free allocation before CF applies.
- **Conditionality:** energy-efficiency audits or Climate Neutrality Plans required; failure → **20% penalty** on remaining free allocation.

### III. Export vulnerability
- **Domestic market:** CBAM levels playing field for EU sales.
- **Global exports:** no CBAM rebate on non-EU sales — EU producers face 100% EUA cost where competitors face zero carbon price.

---

## EUA market dynamics

- More allowances shift from free distribution to **government auctions**.
- LRF simultaneously shrinks total cap → progressive supply tightening toward 2030.
- Secondary market liquidity shifts from spot to **forward hedging**.
